Your own AI operating system.

A training journey for one person. It ends with an operating system of your own: installed on your machine from an open kit, onboarded to you, with its first routine running.

Start with a conversation

One person at a time.

Who it is for

One person. A professional or an owner who already uses AI every day, simply, mostly from the phone, and wants it to work as a system rather than as a chat.

Not a team, not a department. A company that wants a rollout starts with the NHNCD AI Operating System. This page is for the person who wants their own.

«One person. Not a rollout.»

You use AI every day.

Simply, and mostly from your phone. You have felt what it can do, and what it gets wrong.

You want a system, not a chat.

Reminders, drafts, sorting, the day paced. Work that runs on files you own, not on what you remember to ask.

You learn by reading.

Written explanation and diagrams over lecture video. Principles in depth, no equations. Exercises that test understanding.

You have a few hours a week.

Fewer, longer sittings rather than daily practice. Bottom-up, never assuming a level you do not have.

The journey

Four steps. It starts as a conversation, not a syllabus. A sitting draws out who you are, how you use AI today and how you learn. A written plan follows, for your corrections. Weekly sittings work through it, principles first. The capstone is your own operating system, installed and onboarded with you.

Sitting Plan Sittings Capstone
01

The sitting

About two hours. Who you are, how you use AI today, why now, what you want to be able to do, how you learn, and what your employer allows.

You leave with a written record, minutes you can share, and one piece of homework: note every repetitive task you meet during the week.

02

The plan

Written for you from the sitting. Sent within the week, for your corrections.

It fixes the order and the pace. Nothing goes into it that has not been checked first.

03

The sittings

Weekly, in longer blocks. Prompts first, then goals, then the ecosystem around you: context, connections, capabilities, cadence.

Mechanisms matter more than exercises. Exercises are there to test understanding, not to fill the week.

04

The capstone

Your own operating system. Installed on your machine from an open kit, onboarded from seven questions, seeded from your own sittings.

What you leave with

Four things, and all four are yours.

An operating system of your own

In a folder on your machine and in a repository you own. The kit is free and open. Your account, your sign-ins, no other credential.

Yours

Onboarded to you

Your context, your voice, your priorities, your connections, written into it from your own answers. The first thing you ask it: what should I focus on this week?

Onboarded

A first routine

One recurring task off your plate, chosen from your homework list and built with you. It works by hand before it runs on its own.

Live

The rhythm

Before a call, an interview that pulls the plan out of your head. After a call, the record. At the end of a session, the handoff. On day seven the audit, on day fourteen the level-up, then weekly.

Learned

Personal material runs on your own plan, with training on your data switched off. Anything that carries your employer's or a third party's data needs their policy and a commercial account with a data-processing addendum.

What it is not

Not a course.

No fixed syllabus. One plan, written from your sitting, corrected by you, paced to how you learn.

Not a licence.

The kit is free and open. Your subscription is your own. The journey and the installation are the work, not the software.

Not autonomy.

Nothing leaves or gets deleted without you. The system asks before it writes outside its folders. You learn it before it runs anything on its own.

Your files and accounts stay yours.

The system lives in your folder and your repository. Every connection uses your own sign-in. Recordings and records stay on your machine.

What you say in confidence during the sitting stays out of every document.

Your work accounts stay out.

Unless your employer's policy allows them. You ask for the policy; the usual answer is company tools only.

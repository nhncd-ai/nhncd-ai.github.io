## Step 2: grammar, signature move, fingerprint gate

**Grammar: chaptered editorial.** Four chapters, each on its own ground, hard cuts, no drift, no fixed bar (the site's static header stays at the top of the document as on /energy/), the hero a title page in type on the page's own ground with no media above the fold, the close a masthead plate. Why the other seven lost: filmic one-shot forbids hard cuts and LC asked for distinct scenes; live surface forbids marketing chrome and the site's header and headings are required; continuous world needs a single unbroken flight, the opposite of distinct scenes; typographic poster forbids the diagram that carries the signature move; gallery is for a range of objects and this is one argument; split stage holds two columns in tension for the whole page and the journey has four acts, not two sides; rhythmic cutlist is a pulse and LC asked to start low and quiet.

One departure from the grammar's close pattern, deliberate: the call to action is the site's own button component (`.btn`), not a line of running text, because the site's design system wins over the skill's grammar wherever they disagree (the handoff's rule: the engine adapts to the site's tokens, never the reverse). The mailto is also set as running text beside it.

**Signature move: the dots that connect.** Eleven dots, one per artifact of the day, drawn in the site's aperture-dot shape. Act 1: scattered across three parallax planes behind and in front of the title. Act 2: the same eleven dots travel onto one horizontal line as the section crosses the viewport, driven from the act's `--sc-p`. Act 3: the same line, with five numbered landings named along it. Act 4: the same line becomes the day; scroll draws the line and lights each dot with its time and its artifact, and the clock reads the time of the last lit dot. A small folio rail in the right margin (desktop only) repeats the move at page scale: four marks, one per chapter, joined by a hairline that fills as the chapters pass; each mark is a link to its chapter. On phones the rail is hidden and the in-scene dots carry the whole meaning.
